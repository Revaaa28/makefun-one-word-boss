# Candidates — One Word for the Boss

Competition: `u-swift-badger-44-one-word-for-the-boss-6b48c735`
Judge brief criteria, in stated order:
1. Single word a person would actually use about the boss they report to.
2. Funny on its own — a colleague repeats it at lunch because it lands.
3. Rings true — catches a real habit of bosses the entrant has clearly met.
4. Fresh — not the first word anyone would reach for; surprise earns a laugh.
5. Travels — works for a boss in a kitchen, a warehouse or an office.

Selection method: MANUAL ranking by me against the criteria in their stated
order, plus a pairwise check against strong rival-style words. No Gemini
judge, no NVIDIA judge, no judge-API/proxy tournament was used.

## Manual ranking (criteria applied in order)

**Criterion 1 (single word, natural usage):** all 15 pass — each is one word a
person would actually say about their boss. No eliminations here.

**Criterion 2 (funny on its own):** strongest — Goldfish, Seagull, Boomerang,
Blackhole, Teflon, Magician. Weakest — Bottleneck (true but dry, more a
complaint than a laugh), Thermostat (clever but needs a beat to land),
Foghorn (one-note loud joke).

**Criterion 3 (rings true):** strongest — Goldfish (the boss who forgets
approvals/decisions is universal), Teflon (blame never sticks), Boomerang
(rework loops), Seagull (the fly-in manager). Weakest — Magician (weekends
disappearing is true of the *job*, less of the boss's habit), Sunflower
(true but gentle, more sad than funny).

**Criterion 4 (fresh, not first-reach):** Goldfish, Teflon, Boomerang,
Thermostat, Weathervane, Sunflower are all far from the first-reach pile
(Micromanager / Tyrant / Dictator / Overlord / Narcissist / Vampire).
Seagull is slightly less fresh — "seagull manager" is an established office
joke term, so the surprise is thinner. Blackhole and Octopus are familiar
metaphors, still fresh enough as boss words.

**Criterion 5 (travels):** Goldfish, Teflon, Boomerang, Blackhole, Chameleon
travel everywhere. Seagull travels too. Thermostat/Weathervane travel but
lean office-flavoured.

## Pairwise check vs strong rival-style words

The field will be thick with first-reach words: Micromanager, Tyrant,
Dictator, Overlord, Narcissist, Vampire, Ghost, Workaholic, Clueless.
Against those, any of my top 5 wins on criterion 4 (freshness) alone —
the judge's own brief says to decide at the *first* criterion where two
words differ, and freshness is where the clichés lose.

Head-to-head among my own top 5:
- Goldfish vs Teflon: Teflon is clever, but it leans on knowing the
  "Teflon" political idiom; Goldfish memory is understood by everyone
  instantly, and the habit (forgetting what was agreed) is the more
  universal boss experience. Goldfish wins.
- Goldfish vs Boomerang: Boomerang is strong, but rework coming back is
  one kind of boss; forgetting is every kind of boss, every week.
  Goldfish wins.
- Goldfish vs Seagull: Seagull needs the office-joke term to fully land —
  the brief penalises words that need a caption. Goldfish stands alone.
  Goldfish wins.
- Goldfish vs Blackhole: Blackhole is vivid but leans on scale/dread;
  Goldfish is funnier (small, harmless-looking fish, devastating memory).
  Goldfish wins on criterion 2.

## Winner: **Goldfish**

Runner-up: Teflon. If a v2 were ever needed, Teflon is the standby — but
this task is one entry only, so Goldfish stands.
